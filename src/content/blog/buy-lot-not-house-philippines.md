The instinct when buying real estate is to buy a house. You can see it, touch it, move in immediately. But for a growing number of Filipino families — especially those building long-term wealth — buying a **lot first** makes significantly more financial sense. Here's why.

## 1. Lower Entry Price, Same Location Advantage

A residential lot in a well-located subdivision costs a fraction of what a fully built house in the same area would cost. You get the same address, the same infrastructure, the same NLEx access — at a fraction of the price.

That lower entry point means:
- Smaller reservation fee and down payment
- More flexible payment terms
- Less financial strain in the early years
- More capital left over to build on *your* timeline

## 2. You Build What You Actually Want

A pre-built house reflects someone else's decisions: the builder's preferred layout, the developer's standard finishes, the floor plan that was easiest to replicate 50 times. When you build on your own lot, you decide the layout, the number of rooms, the garage size, the materials.

Your parents can have a ground-floor bedroom. Your home office can actually be the right size. The kitchen can face where you want it to.

Pre-built houses are compromises. Lots are blank canvases.

## 3. Land Appreciates. Structures Depreciate.

This is the financial reality that most buyers don't think about: a house is a depreciating asset. The structure ages, requires maintenance, and eventually needs significant renovation or replacement. The **land underneath** is what appreciates.

When you own the lot, the appreciating asset is yours entirely. Every peso that the location gains in value goes directly to your net worth — not to a developer who sold you both the land and the structure at a bundled price.

## 4. Pre-Selling Prices Are Time-Limited

Developers offer the best prices during pre-selling — before construction is complete and before the subdivision fills up. Once the community is established and remaining lots are scarce, prices go up. The families who bought early lock in the best entry price and capture the most appreciation.

Waiting until you're "ready to build" means paying more for the lot. Buying the lot now and building later is usually the smarter sequence.

## 5. It's a Hedge Against Inflation

Real estate — specifically land — is one of the most effective hedges against inflation available to ordinary Filipino families. As the peso weakens and construction costs rise, the land you already own becomes proportionally more valuable.

A lot purchased today at pre-selling prices, held for five to ten years, is a tangible, appreciating asset that no bank account or mutual fund can fully replicate.

## Is a Lot Right for You?

A lot makes the most sense if:
- You want to build eventually but aren't ready to start yet
- You want to invest without the management complexity of a rental unit
- You want to leave something concrete for your children
- You want the best price in a growing area before it peaks

It makes less sense if you need to move in immediately and don't have the budget or timeline to build.
